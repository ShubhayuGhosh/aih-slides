# Autoimmune hepatitis: teaching revision

Audience: undergraduate classmates. Suggested main-talk duration: 12–15 minutes; this is a pacing target, not a measured runtime. The main sequence has 16 slides plus references/Q&A. Three backups are optional. The seven-stage pathogenesis animation is preserved.

## Presentation controls

- Arrow keys / Space: reveal the next step or advance.
- N: speaker notes, including likely questions and short answers.
- B or the Backup button: open backup slides; press again to return to the slide you left.
- End: references/Q&A in the main talk, final backup while in the appendix.
- The normal forward sequence stops at references. Topic links there open individual backups.

## Content decisions

This is a standard internal-medicine teaching structure, checked against society teaching resources, guidance and original diagnostic/histological publications. No particular textbook or edition was supplied; no claim of page-by-page textbook concordance is made.

- Move geographic incidence charts out of the main talk. Prioritise recognition and type comparison.
- Sex comparison uses the **specific AASLD 2024 teaching table**: approximate female:male ratios 4:1 for type 1 and 10:1 for type 2. These are not universal constants; other references/cohorts differ. Keep the slide citation and the qualification in notes. The overall “3 in 4” estimate refers to AIH overall, a different population from that subtype table.
- Keep the traditional types for undergraduate teaching. Explain in notes that EASL 2025 does not recommend autoantibody-based subclassification of adults for clinical management. Do not equate antibody type with a fixed prognosis. Anti-SLA/LP is not restricted to type 1 or an established separate “type 3”.
- Keep the seven pathogenesis scenes, with shorter captions. Mark environmental triggers and molecular mimicry as proposed explanations. Avoid describing a uniform Treg deficiency or antibody-mediated hepatocyte killing as established in every patient.
- Add clinical signs and distinguish injury (ALT/AST) from function/severity (especially INR in acute illness).
- Show diagnostic reasoning before the calculator. Normal IgG, negative antibodies and a low simplified score do not independently exclude AIH.
- Label the calculator as the **original adult 2008 score**. Its historical “typical” biopsy definition requires the constellation including rosettes AND emperipolesis, unlike modern pattern-based histology assessment. Antibody points are capped at two.
- Correct the histology consensus reference to **Liver International**, not Journal of Hepatology. Explain that rosettes and emperipolesis are not specific.
- Distinguish competing diagnoses from coexistence: MASLD and AIH can occur together. Drug-induced autoimmune-like hepatitis is not automatically idiopathic AIH, and checkpoint-inhibitor injury is not automatically classical AIH.
- Give treatment and follow-up separate slides. Explain rapid induction versus slower steroid-sparing maintenance; target normal AST, ALT and IgG.
- Identify EASL 2025 changes explicitly: MMF is an adult first-line alternative; budesonide is no longer a first-line recommendation. Keep detailed adult doses in backup A and do not extrapolate them to children.
- Withdrawal after two years of stable biochemical remission is a possible specialist decision in selected patients, not an automatic end date. Relapse and monitoring remain central.
- Replace automatic “list for transplant after seven days” with urgent specialist assessment, early reassessment and escalation according to deterioration/response. Do not defer referral in liver failure.
- Retain old treatment trials as historical backup evidence, not a modern survival forecast.

## Academic content revision — 6 November 2026

Scope: wording and clinical substance. The stylesheet, navigation logic, geometry, materials, animation timings and seven-stage sequence are unchanged. The only changes inside the 3D implementation are label strings.

The language now follows an undergraduate medical-seminar register. Metaphorical headings, slogans, conversational prompts and instructions about how to explain the content have been replaced by clinical terminology and concise interpretation.

Substantive changes:

- Define AIH using its inflammatory phenotype and characteristic biochemical, serological and histological constellation; describe its progression and treatment response.
- Replace vague epidemiology statements with the classical age distribution, an explicitly pooled global incidence, and recognised autoimmune associations.
- Add IgA deficiency to the traditional type comparison, retaining the explicitly attributed sex ratios and classification caveat.
- Restore HLA class II antigen presentation, Th1/Th17 signalling, cytotoxic pathways, plasma-cell differentiation and stellate-cell activation to the mechanism sequence. Describe molecular mimicry and microbial translocation as proposed mechanisms. Restrict the AIRE example to APECED.
- Replace generic presentation labels with sourced acute-presentation and autoimmune-comorbidity ranges. Attribute cirrhosis at diagnosis to the Danish cohort with the correct biopsy denominator.
- Give laboratory interpretation its own role: hepatocellular pattern, IgG, limitations of antibodies, and severity markers. The 10% normal-IgG estimate is attributed to Hartl's 1,318-patient cohort.
- Give interface and lobular inflammation greater prominence in histology. Retain the definitions of rosettes and emperipolesis in notes, including their limited specificity and their historical role in the 2008 score.
- Put adult induction and azathioprine maintenance ranges on the core treatment slide.
- Define four-week non-response and six-month complete biochemical response. Explicitly distinguish IAIHG 2022 time points from EASL 2025's 6–12-month assessment window.
- Distinguish corticosteroid tapering from cessation of all immunosuppression. EASL 2025 permits consideration after at least two years of stable response on low-dose monotherapy in selected patients; BSG 2025 suggests considering steroid-sparing withdrawal after 3–4 years of sustained response.
- Clarify acute severe AIH versus AIH-related acute liver failure, postpartum relapse risk, and cirrhosis surveillance.

Additional source checks:

- [Hartl et al., Features and outcome of AIH patients without elevation of IgG, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7139106/): 130 of 1,318 patients had normal IgG at presentation.
- [Grønbæk et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24326217/): 1,721 patients in the cohort; 28.3% of 1,318 biopsied patients had cirrhosis at diagnosis.
- [IAIHG response consensus, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35066089/): four-week non-response and six-month biochemical-response definitions; histological remission mHAI <4/18.
- [EASL 2025](https://easlcampus.eu/sites/default/files/2025-08/EASL_CPGs_on_the_management_of_AIH.pdf): adult induction/maintenance, monitoring and withdrawal. Read alongside the [BSG 2025 guideline](https://gut.bmj.com/content/74/9/1364) because some recommendations differ.
- [van Gerven et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25611558/): HLA-DRB1*03:01 and *04:01 associations in type 1 AIH; neither is required for disease.
