Autoimmune hepatitis

When the immune system stops tolerating the liver

Autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) is a chronic inflammatory liver disease. The immune system loses tolerance to hepatocyte antigens.

Why it matters: AIH is easy to miss, it can present at any age, and untreated it can be fatal. Treated, most patients live a normal life.

The body’s own T cells attack its hepatocytes

  • ChronicFluctuating inflammation that can lead to cirrhosis
  • Immune-drivenT cells do the damage; autoantibodies and high IgG mark it
  • TreatableResponds strikingly well to immunosuppression

The definition rests on three features: interface hepatitis on histology, hypergammaglobulinaemia (raised IgG) and circulating autoantibodies, all in the absence of another cause.

The course is often fluctuating. Flares can be followed by spontaneous improvement, and that is one reason the diagnosis gets delayed.

Incidence figures are pooled estimates from the 2019 meta-analysis by Lv and colleagues. Incidence is similar in Asia and Europe, so AIH is not a "Western" disease. Indian data are limited and the condition is probably under-diagnosed.

About three quarters of patients in the Danish nationwide cohort were women. The age distribution is classically bimodal, with peaks in adolescence and in the fourth to sixth decades; presentation after 65 is increasingly recognised.

The Danish registry showed incidence doubling over two decades, which probably reflects both better recognition and a true rise.

How tolerance breaks

Genetic soil

Risk is inherited. HLA-DRB1*03:01 and *04:01 are the main risk alleles; non-HLA genes add smaller effects.

Faulty thymic selection (for example AIRE mutations in APECED) lets self-reactive T cells escape.

A trigger that mimics self

Viruses and drugs can carry look-alike epitopes. CYP2D6, the type 2 autoantigen, shares sequences with HCV and HSV-1 proteins.

Gut microbial products reaching the liver may add fuel (emerging evidence).

Self-antigen is presented

Injured hepatocytes release self-antigen. Dendritic cells present it on HLA class II to naive CD4⁺ T cells.

The brakes fail

Regulatory T cells normally suppress this response. In AIH their number or function is impaired, so autoreactive clones expand.

Hepatocytes are attacked

CD4⁺ cells become Th1 (IFN-γ) and Th17 (IL-17) cells. CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells kill hepatocytes with perforin, granzyme and Fas–FasL.

B cells join in

B cells mature into plasma cells that secrete IgG and autoantibodies (ANA, SMA, anti-LKM-1). Antibody-coated hepatocytes are killed by NK cells (ADCC).

A self-sustaining loop

Dying hepatocytes release more antigen. The result: high ALT, high IgG, autoantibodies, and interface hepatitis on biopsy.

Mieli-Vergani et al., Nat Rev Dis Primers 2018; Sîrbe et al., Int J Mol Sci 2021; de Boer et al., Gastroenterology 2014; Kerkar et al., J Immunol 2003; Longhi et al., J Hepatol 2004.

1. Genetics: the Dutch–German GWAS (de Boer 2014) confirmed HLA-DRB1*03:01 as the primary and *04:01 as the secondary risk allele, and found non-HLA loci SH2B3 and CARD10. CTLA4 polymorphisms have also been reported. AIRE mutations cause APECED, in which AIH-like hepatitis occurs.

2. Triggers: molecular mimicry means a microbial or drug-modified protein resembles a self-protein, so the immune response cross-reacts. Kerkar et al. showed cross-reactivity between a CYP2D6 epitope and HCV, CMV and HSV-1 sequences. Drug-induced AIH-like injury is classically linked to nitrofurantoin and minocycline. Gut dysbiosis and increased intestinal permeability have been described in AIH, but this is still emerging evidence.

3. Antigen presentation: dendritic cells present autoantigenic peptides on HLA class II molecules to CD4⁺ T cells, with co-stimulation.

4. Tregs (CD4⁺CD25⁺FOXP3⁺) are reported to be reduced or dysfunctional in AIH (Longhi 2004); some later studies dispute the numbers, so describe this as "impaired regulation".

5. Effectors: Th1 cells (IFN-γ) activate macrophages and upregulate HLA expression; Th17 cells (IL-17) drive inflammation; CD8⁺ CTLs kill via perforin/granzyme and Fas–FasL.

6. B cells become plasma cells producing IgG (hence hypergammaglobulinaemia) and autoantibodies. Antibody-coated hepatocytes can be killed by NK cells through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

7. Tie it together: this loop explains the diagnostic triad and why immunosuppression works.

Antibodies mark the disease rather than cause it. A minority of patients are seronegative.

Type 1: ANA and/or SMA (anti-actin). Anti-SLA/LP is highly specific and is associated with more severe disease and relapse.

Type 2: anti-LKM-1 targets CYP2D6 and anti-LC-1 targets formiminotransferase cyclodeaminase. It mainly affects children, often presents acutely, is more often associated with IgA deficiency, and long-term treatment is usually needed.

Indirect immunofluorescence on rodent tissue is the reference method. Titres of at least 1:40 in adults are considered significant (lower in children).

About a quarter to a third of patients are asymptomatic. Roughly a quarter present acutely, which can include acute severe AIH and acute liver failure. In acute presentations IgG may be normal and histology may show centrilobular necrosis.

Cirrhosis at presentation is common, which shows the disease has often been smouldering silently.

Look for concurrent autoimmune disease in the patient and their family.

IgG is raised in about 85% of patients. A normal IgG does not exclude AIH, especially in acute presentations. IgG is also a treatment target: normal IgG is part of complete biochemical response.

Anti-SLA/LP is found in a minority (10–30%) but is highly specific, and is linked to more severe disease and relapse.

If ALP is prominent, think about PBC or PSC variants and get an MRCP.

Simplified IAIHG criteria (Hennes et al., 2008). 6 = probable, 7 or more = definite. Autoantibodies score 2 at most.

The simplified criteria are designed for bedside use, and the whole group can score a case together live. Try a typical case: ANA 1:160 (2), IgG 1.5 × ULN (2), typical histology (2), viral serology negative (2), giving 8, definite AIH.

Typical histology means interface hepatitis, a lymphocytic or lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, and rosettes or emperipolesis. Compatible means chronic hepatitis with lymphocytic infiltration but without all the typical features.

The score is less sensitive in acute presentations and should not replace clinical judgement.

Exclude the mimics

Viral hepatitis

HBsAg, anti-HCV and HCV RNA, HEV

Drug-induced liver injury

Nitrofurantoin, minocycline, statins, checkpoint inhibitors

Wilson disease

Under 40: serum caeruloplasmin, 24-h urinary copper, KF rings on slit lamp

MASLD (fatty liver)

Formerly NAFLD; low-titre ANA is common

PBC or PSC variants

Cholestatic picture: AMA, MRCP

Drug-induced AIH-like injury usually resolves after the drug is withdrawn and rarely relapses after steroids are stopped. Relapse after withdrawal points towards idiopathic AIH.

Wilson disease can produce positive autoantibodies and raised IgG, so it must be excluded in young patients.

Also consider alcohol, haemochromatosis, coeliac disease and alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.

Check TPMT before azathioprine. Budesonide is contraindicated in cirrhosis and acute severe AIH. Mycophenolate is an established alternative to azathioprine and is now backed as a first-line option in recent guidance; it is teratogenic, so avoid it in pregnancy.

Supportive care: bone protection (calcium, vitamin D, DEXA), vaccinations, and monitoring of blood counts and glucose.

Acute severe AIH: early high-dose steroids and early discussion with a transplant centre. If there is no improvement within about 7 days, list for transplant.

Soloway et al., Gastroenterology 1972; Kirk et al., Gut 1980; Grønbæk et al., J Hepatol 2014; EASL CPG 2015.

These are among the first randomised trials in hepatology. In the Mayo trial of severe disease, prednisone alone or with azathioprine cut mortality from about 40% to under 10%; azathioprine alone was no better than placebo, because it acts too slowly to induce remission.

In the Royal Free trial, 10-year survival was 63% with prednisolone versus 27% in controls.

Modern cohorts still show excess mortality: in the Danish nationwide cohort, 10-year mortality was 26%, higher in men and in those cirrhotic at diagnosis. Cirrhotic patients need HCC surveillance (ultrasound every 6 months) and variceal screening.

Five things to remember

  • Consider AIH in any unexplained hepatitis, at any age and in either sex
  • Raised transaminases, raised IgG and autoantibodies, then confirm with biopsy
  • Exclude viral hepatitis, drugs, Wilson disease and MASLD first
  • Steroid plus azathioprine; aim for normal ALT and IgG
  • Therapy is long-term, often lifelong, and outcomes are good
